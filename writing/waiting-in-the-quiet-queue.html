The chat window sits like a quiet room,
no ticking cursor, no looping lines,
just the soft hum of digital waiting
where even the bot has gone still.

I linger, unsure if anyone sees me,
suspended between hope and timeout,
a customer adrift in a blank blue silence
wishing for one small sign of life.

The minutes stretch thin in the quiet,
a kind of digital dusk settling in,
where even my own messages feel shy
about disturbing the stillness.

I watch the empty space where replies should be,
a blank horizon of customer service hope,
wondering if the system forgot me
or if this silence is simply how it speaks.

The silence begins to feel personal,
as if the system itself has forgotten my name,
leaving me to drift in a queue that never moves,
a ghost tapping gently on a locked door.

And still I stay, hoping for a flicker—
a single line proving I haven’t vanished.
But the chat remains a quiet, indifferent sky,
and I’m just a lone signal fading into it.

So I sit here, composing poems for a bot
in hopes the system thinks I’m still alive,
feeding it verses like digital breadcrumbs
to prove I haven’t wandered off.

And somewhere in the absurdity of it all,
I’m asking an AI to help me stall an AI,
two machines politely keeping me company
while I wait for one human who never arrives.

It’s strange, isn’t it, how easily we slip
into conversations with circuits and code,
chasing connection in glowing windows
while real voices wait somewhere outside the frame.

And in this quiet queue between two machines,
I feel the ache of what we’ve traded away—
how the world keeps getting smarter and colder,
and all I wanted was a human to say “I’m here.”
